# Task sheets and facilitator script

Rule for the facilitator: **say less than you want to.** Do not explain a rule, a button or a clue. Use only the neutral prompts below.

## Part A. Before the game
1. How often do you read, watch or listen to history? (weekly or more / sometimes / rarely / never)
2. Which daily puzzles or quizzes do you play, and how often?
3. What phone are you using? Do you use larger text, a screen reader or reduced motion?

## Part B. Think-aloud warm-up (2 minutes)
Open any news site. "Please find tomorrow's weather and say aloud everything you are looking at and thinking."

## Part C. First screen
Open `?study=Pnn&cond=X` on the participant's phone. Do not tap anything.
- "Before you touch anything, what do you think this game is, and what would you do first?"
- "What do you think Insight is?" (note the words used)

## Part D. Play
"Please play the day. Say what you are thinking, including anything confusing or annoying."

Prompt ladder when stuck for 60 seconds. Use in order, record the highest rung used.
1. "What are you thinking about right now?"
2. "What do you think the next step could be?"
3. "What do you see on the screen that might help?"
4. Point at an area without naming it: "Have you looked here?"
5. Tell them what to press. (Counts as a failed task.)

Fixed probes (ask at the moment, keep each under 30 seconds):
| When | Probe |
|---|---|
| After the first clue purchase | "What did that cost you, and what did you get for it?" |
| After the first lock, right or wrong | "How do you feel about that result?" |
| After the first miss | "Was that fair? What would you do differently?" |
| When Insight first looks low | "How much do you have left, and can you get more?" |
| After case 5 | "How are you finding this so far? Tired, bored, curious?" |
| After case 10 | "What would you do now?" (record verbatim) |

If a participant wants to stop, let them, record the case number, and go to Part E.

## Part E. Comprehension quiz (no looking back at the screen)
Score each: right / partly / wrong. Correct answers in brackets.
1. "Can you get more Insight during the day?" [No. It never refills.]
2. "Is Insight for one case or for all ten?" [All ten, one shared pool.]
3. "What happens if you lock a wrong year?" [You see the answer, you cannot try again in that case, the day continues.]
4. "The clues say 'by the end of that year'. What does that mean?" [True on 31 December of the target year.]
5. "If a clue turns out to tell you nothing new, what does it cost?" [Nothing.]
6. Condition B only: "What does a Key link clue do?" [It gives the connecting rule that picks the year out of the remaining possibilities.]

## Part F. Scales and interview
Each on 1 (not at all) to 5 (very much). Read the item, do not explain it.
- The game was fair.
- A wrong answer felt like my own mistake.
- Ten cases was too many.
- I understood what Insight is for.
- I felt I needed to know history to do well.
- I enjoyed it.
- I would play again tomorrow.

Open:
- "What was the most confusing moment?"
- "When did you feel clever? When did you feel cheated?"
- "Did you feel you had enough information in the first screen of each case to make a guess?"
- "What would you tell a friend this game is?"
- "What would you want to do right after finishing?"

## Part G. Trace probe and other condition
Read exactly: "Imagine a tool where you pick a year and the game tells you whether the answer is earlier or later than that year. It costs 2 Insight. Would you want that? Would you use it? Instead of what?" Record the words. Do not show a picture.
Then show the other condition for one case, read-only: "Which of these two would you rather play, and why?"

Thank the participant. Download the log. Clear site data.

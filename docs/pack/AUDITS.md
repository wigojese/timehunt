## 3. Clue leakage

Machine checks, all passing: (a) no free text, anchor label, anchor date, title, question or pre-purchase label prints the target year; (b) every paid clue keeps the target; (c) every paid clue removes at least one year from C0; (d) |C0| is 3 to 6 in all ten cases (sizes 3, 6, 5, 5, 3, 3, 3, 6, 6, 3); (e) the free relation selects exactly the target in all ten; (f) no clue prints another case's target year.

**Findings the linter does not fail on, but a reviewer should weigh.**

1. **Every case is solvable by arithmetic from free evidence alone.** The free relation ("one calendar year after X") names an anchor with a stated date, so a player can add and finish without historical knowledge. This is what the Stage 3 brief asked for (a novice solves from free evidence) and what D-019 requires, but it has a cost: **paid Insight has no job in these ten cases.** The Stage 2 economy assumed 3 to 6 candidates left after the free dossier. Here the effective residual after reading everything is 1. This is the largest open question of the stage (Q-32).
2. **Resolving paid clues.** 2 paid clues leave a single candidate (case 1 p2, case 6 p2). 7 paid clues print the target year in their own text (case 1 p2, case 2 p2, case 3 p1, case 4 p1, case 5 p1, case 6 p2, case 10 p1). Once bought, those clues name the answer. That is acceptable for a clue that resolves, but the player should not be able to mistake a purchase for a hint when the price is 2.
3. **Thin ladders.** If the free relation were hidden (the Stage 2 assumption), the residual after buying every paid clue would be case 1: 1, case 2: 2, case 3: 4, case 4: 1, case 5: 1, case 6: 1, case 7: 2, case 8: 3, case 9: 5, case 10: 1. Cases with one paid clue cannot be resolved by purchase alone. Each case needs two or three real paid clues, not one or two.
4. **Case 6 and case 10 share an answer (1977).** The finale's design needs this coincidence, but a player who remembers case 6's answer can try 1977 on case 10 with 1976 and 1978 as the only alternatives. Case 10's paid clue 1 literally restates case 6's answer. Recommended: keep the coincidence, but remove case 10's paid clue 1 and make the finale's relation clue the only route, or move the coincidence to the Trail screen after both are locked.
5. **Pattern risk.** Seven of ten answers have a relation of exactly one calendar year after an anchor (cases 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 10). A player who notices will guess anchor+1. Vary the offsets (the draft already uses 2 in case 6 and 4 in case 8) and add non-integer-offset relations such as ordering or era counting (case 9).
6. **Uncertainty flags.** Cases 5, 8 and 9 involve dates players may know differently (August versus July 1799; 1443 versus 1446; 2 CE versus 'first century BCE'). Each target definition states which event is meant and the miss explanation says what the near answers were.
